Week of Jun 2–8, 2026
Fuel Prices This Week: Biggest Rollback Since April as US-Iran Ceasefire Extends

Motorists get major relief this Tuesday, June 2 with the biggest single-week rollback since the crisis peaked in April. The DOE prescribed a minimum rollback of ₱9.26/L for diesel, ₱4.76/L for gasoline, and ₱10.86/L for kerosene, ending the five-week gasoline hike streak that started in late April.

The catalyst is a major geopolitical development: the United States and Iran have agreed to extend their ceasefire for another 60 days and allow oil tanker traffic to flow freely through the Strait of Hormuz, sending global crude prices sharply lower. DOE Secretary Garin noted the country is now getting closer to pre-war prices, which were around ₱50 to ₱60 per liter.

Real pump prices from GasWatch PH today confirm the rollback, with Metro Manila diesel averaging ₱82.54/L and unleaded at ₱85.88/L across 964 tracked stations. The cheapest diesel today is at Flying V for ₱73.53/L, while the cheapest UNL91 is at Jetti for ₱77.97/L. The country's fuel supply stands at a healthy 45.97 days as of May 29.

After this week's rollback, the year-to-date net increase narrows to ₱44.96/L for gasoline, ₱37.58/L for diesel, and ₱29.64/L for kerosene. Check back next Tuesday, June 9 for the latest adjustments.

§ 04 How It Works

How fuel prices work in the Philippines

A plain-language guide to the DOE weekly pricing cycle and what drives your pump price

Every Tuesday morning, millions of Filipinos check whether fuel prices went up or down. But the mechanism behind those changes, and why they happen so predictably, is little understood. Here's a complete explanation.

Under the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act of 1998 (Republic Act 8479), oil companies in the Philippines are free to set their own prices, and there is no government-mandated ceiling. However, the Department of Energy (DOE) established a voluntary guideline that price adjustments take effect every Tuesday, with announcements made Monday evening.

The Tuesday cycle is not legally required; it is a voluntary industry norm that major players (Shell, Petron, Caltex, Seaoil, Phoenix, Cleanfuel) have followed consistently since deregulation. If a company breaks from this schedule, it is usually because of a very large price movement being staged over multiple days, what the industry calls a "staggered implementation."

Philippine oil companies use the Mean of Platts Singapore (MOPS), a regional benchmark published daily by S&P Global Commodity Insights. The "reference week" is the prior Monday–Friday period. Companies average those five days then add:

Component 1
MOPS Price
Average of the prior week's MOPS assessments. Denominated in USD per barrel.
Component 2
Freight & Insurance
Shipping cost from Singapore to the Philippines, typically $1–3 per barrel.
Component 3
USD/PHP Exchange Rate
The BSP's reference rate for the week. A weaker peso amplifies price increases.

To this "import cost" they add: import duties, customs charges, company margins, and station operating costs. Taxes are applied on top.

The TRAIN law (effective 2018) significantly raised excise taxes on petroleum. Current rates: Diesel ₱6.00/L · Gasoline ₱10.00/L · Kerosene ₱5.00/L · LPG ₱3.00/kg. On top of excise, a 12% VAT is applied to the full pump price. Taxes typically account for ₱15–₱20 of every liter, which is why pump prices don't fall as sharply as international crude when global markets drop.

Most weeks, companies implement the full price change in one move on Tuesday, a "settled" adjustment. When the change is very large (typically more than ₱2.00/L), some companies roll it out in daily increments over 3–7 days, a "staggered" adjustment. Not all brands stagger simultaneously, which creates temporary inter-brand price differences mid-week.

On fuelprice.ph, we mark prices as SETTLED or STAGGERED and update daily during active rollout periods.

All major brands use essentially the same MOPS-based import cost formula, but final pump prices differ because of company margins, additive costs, and brand strategy. Shell's V-Power and Petron's Blaze Racing contain proprietary fuel additives marketed for engine protection, and these come at a price premium. Seaoil and Cleanfuel, which operate fewer stations and have lower overhead, can price more aggressively.

The prices on fuelprice.ph are Philippine advisory prices. Stations in the Visayas, Mindanao, Palawan, and remote island provinces typically charge ₱5–₱25 more per liter for gasoline and diesel, and ₱20–₱80 more per LPG cylinder, reflecting additional shipping, hauling, and storage costs to areas not served by pipelines or large oil depots.

§ 05 Fuel Types

Guide to fuel types in the Philippines

What each fuel is, who uses it, and how its price behaves

Diesel
₱76.25–90.74 / L · Reference pricing

Diesel is the lifeblood of the Philippine economy. Buses, jeepneys, trucks, cargo ships, construction equipment, and farm machinery all run on diesel. It is the single most economically significant fuel product in the country.

Compression-ignition diesel engines don't use spark plugs, as fuel ignites from the heat of compression alone. This gives diesel higher energy density and torque, making it ideal for heavy-duty applications.

Price behavior: Diesel tracks global distillate demand closely. The excise tax (₱6.00/L) is lower than gasoline, keeping it generally cheaper per liter. Diesel Plus adds cleaning additives for modern CRDI engines, typically costing ₱3–₱4 more per liter.

Trucks & Buses Jeepneys Farm Equipment
Gasoline
₱79.89–91.74 / L · Reference pricing

Gasoline powers most private cars and motorcycles. The label number (91, 95, 97) is the RON (Research Octane Number), and higher RON means greater resistance to engine knock.

Unleaded 91 suits most everyday Filipino cars. Premium 95 is for turbocharged or higher-compression engines, and using it in a standard engine provides no benefit. Gasoline carries the highest excise tax at ₱10.00/L.

Private Cars Motorcycles
LPG
₱1,498–₱1,530 per 11kg · Reference pricing

Liquefied Petroleum Gas is the primary cooking fuel for millions of Filipino households. The standard 11kg cylinder is used for everyday cooking; restaurants use the 22kg cylinder.

Price behavior: LPG prices follow Saudi Aramco's monthly Contract Price (CP), not weekly MOPS, so LPG doesn't always move in sync with gasoline and diesel. Excise tax is ₱3.00 per kg. Major brands include Shellane, Gasul, Solane, Caltex, Fiesta, and Isla Gas.

Household Cooking Commercial Kitchens
Kerosene
₱102.18–118.18 / L · Reference pricing

Kerosene is used for cooking stoves and lamps in rural areas without LPG access. It is a middle-distillate product closely related to Jet A-1 aviation fuel, as both are refined from the same crude oil fraction, although Jet A-1 undergoes far stricter refining to meet aviation safety specifications. Not all stations carry kerosene; Shell, Petron, and Caltex are the most reliable sources at major stations nationwide.

Price behavior: Kerosene tracks diesel very closely because both are mid-distillate products. When diesel spikes, kerosene almost always follows within the same week. Excise tax: ₱5.00/L.

§ 06 FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about Philippine fuel prices, our data, and how to use this site

Under the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act (RA 8479), oil companies are free to set their own prices. However, the industry voluntarily follows a DOE guideline of implementing adjustments every Tuesday, with announcements on Monday evening. This weekly cycle is based on the prior week's Mean of Platts Singapore (MOPS) prices, freight rates, and the USD/PHP exchange rate.

The Tuesday schedule was adopted to bring discipline and predictability to a fully deregulated market. The major brands (Shell, Petron, Caltex, Seaoil, Phoenix, Cleanfuel) have adhered to it consistently since 1998.

All major brands use the same fundamental MOPS-based import cost formula, but final pump prices differ because of additive costs, brand strategy, and overhead structure. Shell and Petron invest heavily in proprietary fuel additives that improve engine performance, and those costs are reflected in the pump price. Cleanfuel and Seaoil run leaner operations and pass the savings on to consumers.

Diesel Plus is a premium diesel formulation with enhanced additive packages including detergents (clean fuel injectors), lubricity improvers (protect fuel pumps), and cetane boosters (improve combustion). It is designed for modern CRDI diesel engines. It typically costs ₱3–₱4 more per liter. Check your vehicle manual for the manufacturer's recommendation.

Partially. LPG prices operate in a deregulated but monitored framework. Oil companies are free to set retail prices, but the DOE monitors them closely. The government can intervene under the Price Act (RA 7581) during declared emergencies. In practice, LPG prices closely follow Saudi Aramco's monthly Contract Price (CP). Prices on fuelprice.ph are based on official brand suggested retail prices (SRP), but actual prices at your dealer may differ.

If you have confirmed from official Monday evening announcements that prices will increase on Tuesday, filling up Monday night is financially logical because savings per full tank are typically ₱50–₱200. For confirmed decreases, waiting until Tuesday makes more sense. Always rely on official announcements instead of rumours. For LPG, timing matters less since changes are monthly.

Our prices are official brand advisory prices sourced directly from each oil company and cross-checked against the DOE's weekly monitoring reports. We update every Tuesday (and daily during staggered weeks). What they may not reflect: branch-level promotions, loyalty card discounts (Shell Go+, Petron Smile, Seaoil), or provincial prices (typically ₱5–₱25 higher).

Gasoline typically costs more per liter than diesel in the Philippines primarily because of the higher excise tax (₱10.00/L vs ₱6.00/L). However, during periods of high logistics activity, diesel MOPS can surge and briefly narrow or reverse the gap, as happened during COVID-19 lockdowns when goods movement continued while car fuel demand collapsed.

A staggered adjustment is when a company rolls out a large price change in daily increments rather than all at once, such as ₱1.00 per day over three days instead of ₱3.00 on Tuesday. Staggered periods typically finish within 3–7 days. We mark active staggered implementations with the STAGGERED badge and update them daily.

The prices on fuelprice.ph are national reference advisory prices. Actual pump prices vary by location because provinces typically pay more due to secondary freight costs (tanker trucks or inter-island shipping), smaller storage capacities, and island haul premiums. As a rough guide, cities near major depots (Batangas, Cebu, Davao) are within ₱2–₱5 of reference prices. Rural mainland areas add ₱5–₱10, while remote islands can be ₱15–₱30 higher.

Dubai crude is the primary benchmark for crude oil sold to Asian buyers and heavily influences MOPS assessments. The relationship operates with a one-week lag: a $5/barrel rise typically translates to ₱1.50–₱2.50/liter increase the following Tuesday. Dubai crude and USD/PHP in our live ticker are the two most reliable early indicators of next week's price direction.

Our prices come from three cross-checked sources: (1) Official oil company advisories from Shell, Petron, Caltex, Seaoil, Phoenix, and Cleanfuel every Monday evening or Tuesday morning. (2) The DOE's weekly retail pump price monitoring report. (3) Direct verification from official brand social media and press releases during staggered weeks.

Unleaded 91 (RON 91) is the regular fuel choice for most vehicles in the Philippines. Premium 95 is typically recommended for performance, turbocharged, or high-compression engines that require higher octane. While Premium 95 can safely be used in cars designed for RON 91, it usually won’t improve performance or fuel economy unless your vehicle specifically requires it. For the best fuel option, always check your owner’s manual or the sticker inside your fuel door.

§ 07 Methodology

About our data and methodology

How fuelprice.ph collects, verifies, and publishes fuel price information

01
Source collection
Every Monday evening and Tuesday morning, we collect official price advisories directly from each oil company's website, official social media, and press releases. We do not accept crowd-sourced submissions as a primary source.
02
DOE cross-verification
We cross-check brand advisories against the DOE's weekly retail pump price monitoring report, published every Tuesday. Discrepancies are flagged and resolved before publishing. DOE data covers a wider geographic sample.
03
Staggered tracking
During staggered adjustment weeks, we monitor and update prices daily as each company completes its rollout. Prices are marked STAGGERED until confirmed final for the week, then SETTLED.
04
Market data
Dubai crude and USD/PHP rates in the ticker are sourced from BSP bulletins and publicly available commodity data, providing indicative values that help users understand directional pressure on next week's adjustments.
